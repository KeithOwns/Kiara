1. Breed and coat discussion

We discussed several possible contributors to Kiara’s appearance based on her photos, body proportions, and coat texture. These were visual guesses only, not confirmed ancestry.

Havanese Shih Tzu Maltese Dachshund Spaniel

  • Dachshunds: generally do not have extra vertebrae. Their long-backed look is due mainly to body proportions and shortened limbs, not additional vertebrae.
  • Shih Tzus: often have very soft, silky or cottony coats and prominent long facial hair.
  • Cavalier King Charles Spaniels: also have very soft, silky coats, but typically have cleaner, shorter facial hair around the muzzle than Kiara.
  • Havanese: were highlighted as a particularly plausible visual influence because of Kiara’s very soft coat, long facial furnishings, wavy hair, and small-dog proportions.
  • Maltese: originate from the central Mediterranean, especially Malta, and are part of the old Bichon-type companion-dog family.
  • Havanese relatives: Bichon Frise, Maltese, Bolognese, Coton de Tulear, and other Bichon-type dogs are historically close relatives.
Kiara’s nickname is Kiki. Her coat was described as exceptionally soft—“softer than cotton.”

4. Dog DNA testing

Modern dog DNA tests can be quite useful for identifying major ancestry components, but breed percentages should be treated as estimates rather than exact measurements.

Estimated breed shareHow to interpret it
25–50%+Usually quite convincing with a reputable test.
10–25%Generally useful, though exact percentages can vary by company.
Only a few percentMore tentative; closely related breeds can be confused or grouped differently.

Recommended options

  • Embark Breed ID: the preferred option discussed for mixed-breed ancestry.
  • Wisdom Panel Essential: another reputable choice, often somewhat less expensive.

The underlying DNA reading is highly precise; most uncertainty comes from translating DNA segments into specific breed labels, especially among closely related breeds such as Havanese, Maltese, Bichon-type dogs, or related spaniels.

5. Body proportions and stance

You observed that Kiara’s front legs appear a little longer than her hind legs, her front feet/legs splay outward slightly, and her hips sit a little higher than her shoulders when standing.

  • A mildly rising topline can be seen in Havanese conformation.
  • Outward front-leg splay is not specific to one breed and can appear in small dogs, including dogs with shortened-limb ancestry.
  • If the splay is mild, symmetrical, and she moves comfortably, it may simply be part of her build.
  • If it becomes pronounced, asymmetric, painful, or is associated with limping, it should be examined by a veterinarian.

6. Reproducible “pop” in the left hind leg

You reported that every time you pick Kiara up and hold her close, she extends her hind legs, and when the left hind leg reaches full extension, you can both feel and hear a small pop.

Because the pop is reproducible on the same side at full extension, it is worth a veterinary orthopedic exam rather than assuming it is a harmless joint crack.

Possible causes discussed included:

  • Luxating patella (kneecap slipping in or out of position).
  • A tendon or ligament snapping over a bony prominence.
  • A hip-joint issue or another orthopedic cause.

Until she is examined, avoid deliberately reproducing the pop or allowing that hind leg to hang into full extension.

Signs that should move evaluation sooner

  • Limping or skipping on the leg.
  • Holding the leg up.
  • Crying or showing pain.
  • Reluctance to walk, jump, or use stairs.
  • Weakness or dragging a limb.

7. Proper lifting and one-arm holding

The goal is to support Kiara’s chest and pelvis while preventing the hind legs from dangling into full extension.

Two-hand pickup

  • One hand supports the chest just behind the front legs.
  • The other supports the pelvis/hindquarters.
  • Lift her close to your body and keep her fairly level.

One-arm carry

For the one-arm carry you described, the agreed shorthand was:

Properly saddle both sets of legs.
  • Her front legs can gently straddle your wrist/hand area.
  • Her hind legs can gently straddle your forearm.
  • Your forearm should actually support her body and pelvis; the legs should not simply hang on either side.
  • Keep her close to your torso.
  • Let the hips and knees remain naturally bent and relaxed.
  • Do not deliberately spread her hind legs or let the left hind leg extend straight backward.

A visual aid was also created showing chest support, pelvic support, a level body position, and what to avoid.

8. Running and dog-friendly beaches

You asked about dog-friendly beaches between Malibu and Marina del Rey where you could take Kiki running.

  • Leo Carrillo State Park — North Beach was identified as the main traditionally dog-friendly beach option in the area, with dogs generally allowed on leash in the designated North Beach section.
  • Many major beaches farther south—such as Zuma, Point Dume, Surfrider/Malibu Lagoon, Topanga, Will Rogers, Santa Monica, Venice, and Marina del Rey beaches—generally do not allow dogs on the sand.
Because of Kiara’s reproducible left-hind-leg pop, running her on soft sand was not recommended until a veterinarian evaluates the leg. Soft sand places extra demand on the hips, knees, and stabilizing muscles.

9. Bottom line

  • Kiara’s coat and facial hair make Havanese/Shih Tzu/Maltese-type ancestry plausible.
  • Her body proportions still leave room for Dachshund ancestry.
  • Some spaniel influence remains possible.
  • A reputable DNA test such as Embark or Wisdom Panel would be far more reliable than visual guessing.
  • The recurring left-hind-leg pop should be checked by a vet, and her hindquarters should be supported when she is picked up.
  • For one-arm holding: support her body and let both front and hind legs gently straddle the arm rather than dangle.
